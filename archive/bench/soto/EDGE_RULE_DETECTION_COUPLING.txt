WHY TIGHTENING THE HOMOLOGY EDGE RULE DOES NOT BUY PURITY
2026-08-03

Two independent attempts to improve RNA family grouping failed this week, and they failed by the same
mechanism. That mechanism is a property of the pipeline rather than of either idea, so it is written up
once here and referenced from both.


THE COUPLING

A family must span at least two spatially distinct loci to be emitted at all. So when an edge rule is
tightened and a family loses an edge, the family does not merely become smaller: it can fall below that bar
and disappear, and its copies are then never written out. Detection falls with it.

The consequence is that precision and recall do not trade against each other in the usual way. Tightening
the rule raises precision as intended, but recall falls faster, because whole loci are being removed rather
than only wrong edges.

Measured twice, on the Soto benchmark, with the denominator held fixed at the full truth set:

  lever                                  precision      detected      copies      F1
  identity floor 0.70 -> 0.93          0.796 -> 0.932   296 -> 282   1710 -> 1425   0.438 -> 0.433
  isoform pooling, >=1 -> >=8 exons    0.419 -> 0.523   113 ->  85    545 ->  197   0.494 -> 0.392

In both cases the precision gain is real and arrives exactly as predicted. In both cases the net effect on
F1 is nil or negative.


THE RULE THIS IMPLIES

Any proposal to change the homology edge rule must report DETECTION and COPY COUNT alongside precision. A
proposal evaluated on precision alone, or on a fixed set of already-emitted copies, will look like a win and
be a loss.

The same coupling is why re-grouping a fixed copies.fa is an UPPER bound on such a change and never a lower
one. That error was made on 2026-08-02: an offline re-grouping predicted F1 0.553 for a raised identity
floor, and two full pipeline runs returned 0.433. The offline arithmetic was correct to three decimal
places. The experimental design was not, because it held constant the very thing the change perturbs.


WHAT WAS BEING ATTEMPTED, AND WHAT SURVIVES OF IT

The second attempt is worth recording in full because its premise was correct and remains a live target.

The pipeline keeps one representative transcript per locus and discards the other isoforms. Of the
representatives that cover a known family member, 46 percent are single-exon stubs. At those same loci, 95
percent still have spliced reads and 53 percent carry a spliced chain supported by at least three reads. In
other words a usable spliced model exists, we build it, and then we throw it away. NOTCH2NLA, SRGAP2C and
LIMS1 are all represented by unspliced stubs while 92, 65 and 124 reads respectively support a proper
spliced model at the same locus.

Pooling every isoform's exons into the shared-exon rule was the obvious response, and it does what was
intended: 12.8 times more exons in the pool, and roughly 30 true pairs recovered that the
representative-only rule cannot find. But it never beats the baseline, because it also admits far more false
edges than true ones.

  arm                                precision   recall     F1     new-edge precision
  representative only (baseline)       0.645      0.498    0.562         --
  pooled, one exon >= 100 bp           0.422      0.597    0.494       32/209 = 15%
  pooled, one exon >= 600 bp           0.518      0.591    0.552             25%
  pooled, at least 2 agreeing exons    0.399      0.582    0.473
  pooled, at least 8 agreeing exons    0.523      0.314    0.392

Both refinement axes were tried. Raising the minimum shared length peaks below the baseline. Requiring
several independent exons to agree is worse still, for the coupling reason above.

The honest conclusion is bounded rather than open: about 30 recoverable pairs sit in the discarded isoforms,
and no exon-level rule tested extracts them cleanly. That is a target for a different kind of rule, not an
argument for tuning this one further.


A NOTE ON THE MEASUREMENT HARNESS

These numbers come from a two-chromosome subset, chr1 and chr15, which runs two arms in about 45 minutes
instead of about 3 hours genome-wide. The subset is not arbitrary: those two chromosomes carry the most
stub representatives and 39 percent of all true pairs, including every flagship case. Restricting to two
chromosomes removes cross-chromosome families, so absolute F1 there is not comparable to the genome-wide
figure. Only the difference between arms is.
