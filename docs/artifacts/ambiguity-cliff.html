The Ambiguity Cliff

Two gene copies can be aligned to each other long before a sequencing read loses the ability to tell them apart. This is the distance between those two bars, measured across 3,141 catalog edges — and the second bar turns out to be set by the aligner's scoring matrix, not by us.

Gorilla mGorGor1 · fibroblast Iso-Seq · 3 contigs Catalog arm_f2 · 3,598 loci

The proposition under test

Two criteria, two different questions

Er

Can these two copies be aligned to each other?

Identity ≥ 0.60 and the alignment covers ≥ 0.50 of the shorter sequence. This is what currently defines family membership.

Ec

Can a read land on both, with nothing to choose between them?

Some read has alignments at both loci scoring within 5% of its own best alignment anywhere — AS ≥ 0.95 × best. This currently scopes copy assignment.

The proposal on the table is to drop the first and build the family graph from the second alone, on the grounds that shared multi-mapping reads are “just as good, and less prone to arbitrary thresholds.” Both halves of that are testable. Recomputing Ec on the same loci that produced the shipped Er edges makes the two relations comparable edge by edge, with nothing else changed.

Measurement

Read ambiguity does not rise with identity. It steps.

For each of the 3,141 edges the question is simply whether any read actually lands on both of its loci with a tied score. Grouped by the identity between the two copies, the answer is a flat line and then a wall. If ambiguity were a smooth consequence of similarity, this would be a ramp.

It is not an artefact of how a read is credited to a locus

A placement counts as being “at” a locus when some fraction of its own span falls inside that locus. Mechanism

Why the cliff sits where it does

The location is not an empirical accident, and it has nothing to do with biology. A read of length L aligned to a copy of identity p collects about Lp matches and L(1−p) mismatches, so under match reward A and mismatch penalty B it scores roughly L[Ap − B(1−p)] there, against AL at its own locus. Requiring that ratio to clear a tie threshold τ gives:

Necessary condition for a tie

Two copies of identity p can produce an AS-tied placement of the same read only if

p ≥ (τA + B) / (A + B)

— the aligner's two scoring constants and the tie threshold. Nothing else enters.

The constants are not a matter of opinion. Aligning synthetic reads carrying a known number of mismatches, under this run's exact preset, recovers them directly: A = 1 and B = 4 for splice:hq. The bound becomes p ≥ 0.8 + 0.2τ, and at our tie threshold of 0.95 it lands on p ≥ 0.99.

A second constant works in the same direction one stage earlier. Alignments have to be seeded, and a k = 15 minimizer is shared only if all fifteen bases agree — roughly p15 of the time. At this catalog's median edge identity the second locus is not merely outscored; it is barely seeded.

That bound governs the identity a read locally experiences, and edge identity is measured between whole copies, so it predicts two separate things. Above the bound, ties should be not merely possible but near-universal. Below it, they should occur only where a read happens to sit on a locally more conserved stretch — so they should be sparse, and should not track global identity in any orderly way. Both hold:

Below 0.94 the share wanders between 0% and 24% with no upward trend — it is actually lower at 0.88–0.92 than at 0.80–0.86. Above 0.99 there are no exceptions at all.

The bound is a necessary condition under a uniform-mismatch idealisation. It ignores gap and junction terms, treats mismatches as evenly spread, and applies a read-level quantity to a number measured between full copy sequences. It predicts where ties become possible, not how often they occur — the measurement settles the rest.

The invariance is the damning part. A threshold that buys precision has to change the mix as it rises; this one scales both classes down together. Repeat-driven cross-homology is universal in this genome — 50.5% of random region pairs align at all and 49.0% clear identity 0.60 — and a shared read is precisely the “these two regions contain the same Alu” case. What separates that from paralogy is Er's coverage clause, and read depth is not a substitute for span coverage.

Consequence

A combined gauge is worse than either

The natural compromise — require identity and coverage and a multimapper count — fails on arithmetic before it reaches biology. A conjunction is bounded above by its scarcest term, and multimapper support is by far the scarcest:

The claim about thresholds

The constants move. They do not vanish.

The second half of the proposal is that a shared-read graph is freer of arbitrary cutoffs. It is not. It needs a tie ratio and a minimum shared-read count, and before either of those it inherits whatever the aligner was told:

Er — stated in the definition Ec — inherited from the alignment
identity ≥ 0.60 tie ratio τ = 0.95
coverage ≥ 0.50 of the shorter minimum shared-read count
its own alignment: -k 11 -w 5 -X --no-long-join, forward records only -N 50 — how many alternatives are kept at all
one record per pair; no secondary logic -p 0.1 — whether any alternative is reported
two numbers, written down and swept an implied identity floor (τA+B)/(A+B), never chosen and not reachable from the graph

Both definitions rest on constants — the sequence-homology route needs the aligner too. The asymmetry is in the last row. Er's two thresholds are on the page, defensible, and swept; Ec's decisive one is a consequence of a scoring matrix, and no amount of tuning at the graph level can move it.

Disposition

What this leaves standing

Er defines membership. Ec scopes co-resolution.

They are not competing answers to one question. Sequence homology reaches the of edges where the copies are distinguishable, which is where family structure actually lives. Read conflict reaches the where they are not — which is exactly the set on which assignment has to abstain. The catalog needs both, doing different jobs.

That does not make the multimapper count worthless — it makes it the wrong instrument for this job. What it marks, and marks well, is the set of edges on which no assignment can be honest. Recorded as an optional per-edge annotation it says which pairs of copies a read cannot distinguish, which is precisely the boundary the assign-or-abstain rule needs and precisely what identity alone does not supply. That is the same disposition already taken for cov_longer: disclose it; do not gate on it.

Provenance

Method, and what would overturn this

Ec was recomputed from the read alignments on the identical loci that produced the shipped Er edges, so every number here is a property of one catalog rather than a comparison across two runs. A read contributes a pair when two distinct loci both carry a placement scoring within 5% of that read's best alignment anywhere in the genome. Supplementary alignments are excluded; secondaries are not.

The locus-assignment rule is swept, not chosen. A placement counts as “at” a locus when at least this fraction of its own span falls inside the locus interval. The headline uses the most permissive setting — the one most generous to the shared-read proposal:

  • One substrate, one tissue. Three contigs of one gorilla fibroblast Iso-Seq library. The identity distribution has the same shape genome-wide and in testis, but the cliff itself has been measured only here.
  • Small n at the top. The bins that carry the effect hold tens of edges, not thousands. That is the finding, but it also means the intervals there are wide, so they are drawn rather than suppressed.
  • The derivation is an idealisation. Uniform mismatches, no gap or junction terms, read-level identity substituted for copy-level. It bounds where ties are possible; it does not model their frequency.
  • Absolute counts are protocol-sensitive. An earlier pass over the same question, run under a script that was not preserved, put the overall support at 21.9% rather than , and the cliff band at 82.5% rather than . The band structure and the conclusion are the same; the low-band counts move with the assignment rule. Only the protocol described here is reproducible, and it is the one plotted.
  • What would overturn it. A substrate where Ec support is materially above chance below identity 0.95, or an aligner configuration whose implied floor sits near the catalog median. Either would move the cliff — and the bound says exactly which knob does it.
