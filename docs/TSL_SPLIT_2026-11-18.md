# Assembly recovery by Ensembl transcript support level, human samples (row 1788, 2026-11-18)

Summary page. Pre-registration with its amendment: `PREREG_tsl_split_2026-11-18.md`. Register row 1788. Nothing was
rerun and no rule or default changes.

## Question
User: would the Ensembl transcript support level (TSL) be useful, and where. TSL grades a transcript model by the mRNA
and EST evidence for its structure: 1 = every junction backed by a good mRNA, 2 = best mRNA flagged or several ESTs,
3 = one EST, 4 = best EST flagged suspect, 5 = no support, NA = not analysed.

## Where it can and cannot be used
- Can: as a reporting split of a human benchmark (recovery and misses by level), and to judge lists of missed or novel
  models.
- Cannot: for the apes (human and mouse only), for single-exon models and processed copies (not graded), for copy
  assignment, or inside any assembly or family rule (it would be an annotation crutch).

## Method
Reference: the public CHM13 v2.0 annotation (CAT + Liftoff of GENCODE v35), which carries `transcript_support_level`
per transcript, so no ID join is needed (workspace `r1788_tsl_split/chm13.gene_annotation.gff3`, 4 GB). Unit: a
multi-exon intron chain, at its best level; "new in CHM13" = CAT models with no GENCODE source. Recovered = an assembly
has exactly that chain. Read on expressed genes (a gene where at least one tool recovers at least one chain).
`bench/famcmp/tsl_split.py REF.gff3 OUT.json NAME=arm.gtf ...`.

## Reading
- Level 1 is recovered 5.0 (A119b) and 4.8 (HG002) times as often as level 5: the split separates strong from weak
  reference models.
- Level 5 is recovered about twice as often as level 4 in every tool on both samples: the level is not an ordered scale
  at its low end (levels 3-4 rest on EST evidence). So it is not a testis effect.
- Verdict by the fixed rule: BETWEEN. Reported, not proposed as a standing column of the benchmark tables.
- Predictions: the fall, the weak-miss share and the three HG002 predictions held; "same order of tools at every level"
  and "FLAIR recovers most level 4-5 chains" failed.

## Limits
Human only; mRNA / EST-era evidence, so a low level can mean "not sequenced before"; single-exon models left out; the
reference is the GENCODE lift-over, not the RefSeq truth of the assembly tables (counts not comparable); level and
expression are confounded beyond the expressed-gene restriction; exact intron chains only. The other tools are baselines.
