1. Reads to exons. Primary alignments only (samtools -F 2308: no unmapped, secondary or supplementary
records). An N in the CIGAR is a spliced-out intron, so the blocks between the N operations are the exons.
A D is a deletion, not an intron, and does not split an exon.

2. Reads to candidate transcripts. There are two rules here, not one, and the second builds a large
fraction of everything downstream. Spliced reads: two reads belong to the same candidate if their intron
lists are identical, junction for junction, where a junction is the exact triple (chromosome, donor,
acceptor). Unspliced reads are excluded from that rule entirely - an empty intron list would pool them
chromosome-wide - and are clustered separately, per chromosome, by single-linkage span overlap with no
threshold: any overlap links. That second rule is what produces the single-exon representatives, and they
are 43 percent of the nodes in the NPIP catalogue and 33 percent genome-wide, so it is not a footnote.

A candidate is kept if at least 3 reads support it, but the count is pooled over the LOCUS rather than
counted per candidate: support is summed across the connected component of the junction-incidence graph,
where two candidates are adjacent if they share an exact junction. A 2-read candidate therefore survives
when its locus totals 3 or more. This matters: at DAZ2 twelve reads fragment into nine chains whose best
support is 2, and every one of them would die under a per-candidate threshold. A candidate's start is the
2nd smallest read start and its end the 2nd largest read end, so one runaway read cannot stretch it. One
locus yields many candidates: NPIPB9's 206 reads give 54 distinct intron lists, the largest holding only
21 percent.

3. Candidate transcript to exon-sum. Reference bases at the read-derived exon coordinates, concatenated
(reads give the coordinates, the genome gives the bases). Every junction must be canonical and all
junctions must agree on strand, or the whole candidate is dropped. Canonical means GT..AG, GC..AG or
AT..AC on the plus strand and their reverse complements CT..AC, CT..GC or GT..AT on the minus strand -
six motifs, not two. NPIPB12 has 8 of its 9 junctions canonical and one CT..AT, which is in neither list,
so a 109-read model is discarded. Three plain length bounds also apply at this point: span at most 3 Mb,
and spliced length between 100 bp and 300 kb. Note that a single-exon candidate has no junctions, so this
test is vacuous for it: it passes automatically and its strand cannot be determined this way.

4. Two structural filters, each a conjunction. Drop a single-exon candidate whose span entirely contains
5 or more DISTINCT junctions witnessed in the primary reads, each of them carried by at least 2 reads -
unspliced pre-mRNA lying across a real gene. Distinct rather than total matters: TSPYL1 has 51 junction
observations inside its span but only 4 distinct ones. The rule is read-level, not transcript-level;
phrased over assembled transcripts it misses the RFPL readthrough entirely. Second, drop a candidate with
an intron longer than 50 kb whose exact junction is supported by fewer than 3 primary reads (mis-chain).
Neither of these two drops on length alone, though the three bounds in step 3 are genuine length cut-offs.

5. Candidates to loci. Two candidates are put in the same locus if any of three things holds: (a) they
share at least one identical junction; (b) they are on the same chromosome and strand and their genomic
spans overlap by at least half of the shorter span; or (c) they are on the same chromosome and strand,
their spans overlap at all, and the POA contiguous-core coverage of the shorter exon-sum is at least 0.50 -
which admits alternative-first-exon and alternative-last-exon isoforms whose junction sets are entirely
disjoint. Apply repeatedly until nothing more merges.

Two caveats belong here. First, (b) and (c) measure genomic spans with introns included, so a giant intron
satisfies (b) vacuously: at NPIPB9 the selected representative has 24 aligned blocks, none of them inside
the gene, joined by one 104,410 bp intron that spans the whole of it - membership by absence. Second, the
procedure is deterministic but it is NOT order-independent. Clause (a) is an order-independent union-find,
but (b) and (c) run as a fixed-point loop that re-picks one representative per locus each round and
compares only representatives, so the choice of representative is part of the merge rather than a step
after it. There is no k and no clustering algorithm, but I should not claim the outcome is independent of
ordering, because it is not.

Each locus then keeps one representative: the candidate with the most reads, ties broken by longer span.
NPIPB9's 54 candidates become 1 locus with a 1,546 bp exon-sum. Every other isoform is discarded for the
rest of the pipeline, not temporarily - NPIPA2 has 14 candidates and 6 distinct first exons, and one
survives. This is the largest known information loss in the method: 46 percent of the representatives
covering a known family member are single-exon stubs, and 53 percent of those loci have a discarded
spliced chain carried by 3 or more reads.

6. Loci to the homology graph. All representatives in the run are written to one FASTA, and that same file
is passed to minimap2 as both the target and the query:

    minimap2 -c -X --no-long-join -t <threads> -k 11 -w 5   reps.fa   reps.fa

So the comparison is all-vs-all, not one-vs-all, and it is global rather than per family - there is no
pre-grouping, and families are the OUTPUT of this step rather than an input to it. It runs once, not three
times: the shipped path is single-tier at identity 0.60 or above with the sensitive -k 11 -w 5 seeding.

Two properties of this call are worth stating explicitly. The minimizer index acts as a prefilter, so only
1.86 percent of all possible pairs produced any alignment record at all in the NPIP run; 98 percent of
non-edges are "no alignment found" rather than "failed a threshold". And -X implies --dual=no, so each
pair is emitted in one direction only, (a,b) or (b,a) but never both. Because --dual=no keeps whichever
sequence has the lexicographically smaller FASTA header, and the headers are the representative index, the
query is not necessarily the shorter sequence - measured, it is the longer one in 50.9 percent of records,
essentially a coin flip. Any per-record statistic therefore has to choose its axis explicitly.

An edge exists between two loci if ANY SINGLE alignment record clears all three of: identity (1 - de) at
least 0.60; coverage of at least half of the SHORTER sequence, computed as (qe-qs)/ql when ql <= tl and
(te-ts)/tl otherwise, so the numerator's axis follows the denominator; and forward orientation, since
minus-strand records are dropped. Records are never summed - one qualifying record is enough.

7. Homology graph to final families. Take the connected components of those edges, then refine: any block
of 3 or more nodes whose induced density falls below gamma = 0.20 is recursively split. Blocks of 2 or
fewer nodes are exempt from the test entirely, and the graph gamma runs on is unweighted - identity and
coverage decide whether an edge exists and are then discarded. Gamma is inert on 79 percent of the
catalogue, so for four families in five the block simply is the connected component; it earns its place on
the rest, because without it one component holds 466 copies across 38 families. The operation is
split-only, which is what preserves seed-invariance.

Within a block, two loci that overlap on the same strand are merged back into one unless at least 3 reads
map uniquely (MAPQ above zero) to one of them, which is the evidence that they are genuinely two loci and
not one counted twice. Keep the block as a family only if at least 2 distinct loci remain after that.
