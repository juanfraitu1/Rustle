Rustle × Soto · what "RNA misses a member" means

The genome has all 362 members. RNA recovers 313 — here's why it can't see the other 49

Every missed member is a real gene copy in the genome (DNA finds all 362). RNA misses it for one of a few concrete, mechanical reasons — shown here at the level of the actual reads. It is never that DNA lacks the copy; it is what transcription and splicing do to the signal.

22 not expressed 8 mis-chain 7 K=0 collapse 6 genuine-miss 5 seeding-gap 1 thin 49 total RNA misses

Not expressed 22 members

The copy makes no RNA. You cannot sequence a transcript that was never made.

Many duplicated copies are silent pseudogenes or fixed-but-untranscribed genes. They sit in the genome intact, but produce no transcript — so no read ever lands on them. This is the single biggest reason (22 of 49): not a detection failure, just an absence of RNA.

DNA sees it →The genomic copy is present whether or not it is transcribed, so DNA recovers it. Expression is exactly the axis RNA adds — and loses here.

e.g. SPAG11A, AC097527.1, AC133548.1, DEFB104B …

Mis-chaining 8 members

The aligner stitches one read's exons across different copies — a chimera belonging to none.

Because the copies are ~99% identical over their exons, minimap2 (in splice mode) chains a single read's exons onto whichever copy scores marginally best — exon 1 on NCF1B, exon 2 on NCF1, exon 3 on NCF1C — treating the huge gaps between copies as "introns". But NCF1's real introns are < 5 kb; these "introns" are 234–387 kb — impossible. The read becomes a chimera across three copies, so no single copy assembles a clean transcript, and the whole family collapses to nothing.

DNA sees it →A DNA read is contiguous genomic sequence — there are no "introns" to stitch across copies, and it spans the divergent intron/flank sequence that pins it to one copy. Mis-chaining is a splice-alignment artifact that cannot arise for DNA.

e.g. NCF1 / NCF1B / NCF1C, BOLA2B, TEKT4P2, AC142384.1 …

Can't we just tune the aligner? no knob fixes it

Sweeping minimap2's -G and the other knobs changes the chaining — never the outcome.

We swept 13 knob settings (-G 200 k → 3 k, plus -r, -z, -p, -N, -n, -m). The reads forced local swing from 68 down to 13 — the chaining genuinely changes — but copies resolved stays 0 in every one. Even -G 3 kb, tighter than NCF1's largest real intron (3.45 kb) so it forbids every jump to a paralog, still yields 0 copies; it just throws away coverage.

The tell →A cap that could help would have to sit between the real introns (≤3.45 kb) and the copy distances (0.37–1.5 Mb) — and one does exist — yet even there, 0 copies. So the cap is a red herring: the bottleneck is downstream of the aligner. Once the reads are local, the ~99% identical copies still can't be told apart — the K=0 tie below. Tuning moves where reads land, not whether copies are separable.

K = 0 collapse 7 members

The copies are identical over the exons RNA reads — a read fits both equally, so they merge.

The two copies differ nowhere in the sequence a spliced read covers — there is no variant to say which copy a read came from. Every read is a tie (MAPQ 0), so the copies collapse into one. This is a true identifiability limit, not a bug: the information isn't in the RNA.

DNA sees it →The copies differ in their introns, which RNA splices out. For NCF1 the copies differ at ~2 exonic positions but ~75 genomic positions — a DNA read carries dozens of distinguishing variants; a spliced read carries almost none.

e.g. ANAPC1P1, CASTOR2, TRIM64, H3P4 …

Seeding gap 5 members

Expressed, but too few reads chain locally to build a consensus — a coverage limit, not a fundamental one.

The copy is transcribed, but only one or two reads land on it — below the floor needed to assemble a reliable consensus. Resampling that copy's own reads to full depth recovers it (we verified this with a coverage "top-up"), so it's a depth problem, not an identifiability wall.

DNA sees it →DNA coverage is even across the genome regardless of expression level, so a lowly-expressed copy is fully represented.

e.g. ANKRD36B, DEFB108C / DEFB108D, AC008443.6 … (+ 6 genuine-miss, 1 thin single-exon)

Why DNA recovers all of them

Every one of these is a signal problem, not a genome problem. Not-expressed = no RNA made. Mis-chain = splicing lets the aligner stitch a read across copies. K=0 = splicing removes the very sequence that separates the copies. Seeding-gap = expression is too low. In every case the copy is intact in the genome, which is why the DNA method recovers 361/362.

The through-line: RNA answers "is it expressed and resolvable?", DNA answers "does it exist?" Splicing — the thing that makes RNA informative about isoforms — is also what discards the intronic and flanking sequence needed to tell near-identical copies apart. That trade is the whole story of the 49 misses.
