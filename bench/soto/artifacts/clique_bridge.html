Does a discovery bridge two family cliques?

A copy discovered next to PMS2P reports 100% identity to CNTNAP3 — a different family. If that made a graph edge, it would merge two cliques that must stay apart. It doesn't: the family-edge rule needs id ≥ 0.80 and cov ≥ 0.50, and that 100% hit is a 63 bp fragment (0.8% coverage). Drag the coverage gate and watch what the fragment can and can't do.

What this shows

The two cliques never touch

0 edges qualify between PMS2P and CNTNAP3 at any real threshold. The 100%-identity hit is 63 bp out of 7.4 kb.

The gate does the work

At cov ≥ 0.50 the discovery has 0 qualifying edges — it joins neither clique and bridges nothing. It is a real expressed locus (427 reads), attributed to PMS2P by proximity alone.

Why 0.50, not lower

Drop the gate below ~0.01 and the 63 bp fragment lights up, wiring the discovery into both cliques — a false merge. Coverage is the guard against fragment bridges.

Identity alone is a trap

Ranking by identity put a 63 bp sliver at the top. A family edge is homology over length, not a shared exon or repeat — which is exactly what coverage measures.
