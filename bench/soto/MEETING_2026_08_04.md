# O1 status: what is established, what was refuted

Prepared 2026-08-03. Every number below that is labelled "measured" comes from a pipeline run on the
chr1+chr15 subset (131 Soto truth members, 325 true pairs, 752 locus representatives), scored with a fixed
universe so that members the run never emitted still count against recall. Numbers labelled "edge-level"
were computed on alignment records rather than pipeline output, and the distinction matters: see the
section on the detection coupling.

Scope note. Everything here is O1, which is the question of which loci exist and which of them form a
family. It is a relation: a read may support several sites. O2, assigning a read to the copy that emitted
it, is a separate function and none of this touches it.

---

## 1. The headline change: define a locus by its genomic span

The pipeline used to compare loci by their exon sum, meaning the concatenated exons of one chosen
representative transcript. It now can compare them by the genomic span of the locus instead.

Measured, end to end, both arms on the identical 752 representative node set:

| substrate | copies | families | precision | recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exon sum (previous) | 539 | 132 | 0.514 | 0.606 | 0.556 |
| genomic span | 575 | 127 | 0.527 | 0.658 | 0.585 |

Precision and recall both improve, and the number of emitted copies goes up rather than down. That last
point is the unusual one. Every other change tried this month bought precision by dropping whole loci.

### Why it works

The exon sum is isoform dependent. Two copies of one gene frequently have different representative
isoforms, so their exon sums are non corresponding sequences even when the loci themselves are nearly
identical. Measured median alignment coverage between true family members:

| representative pairing | exon sum | genomic span |
|---|---|---|
| both spliced | 0.259 | 0.914 |
| one single exon | 0.000 | 0.022 |
| both single exon | 0.000 | 0.000 |

The entire gain comes from the both spliced class. Family membership currently depends on which chain
`pick_locus_rep` happens to select, and that is a tie break, not evidence. The genomic span removes it.

This also explains two earlier failures rather than leaving them as unrelated dead ends. Concatenating all
isoforms into one sequence lost 20 recall points because coverage divides by the shorter length, so
lengthening a representative inflated its own denominator. Pooling isoform exons separately avoided that
but admitted false edges at 15 to 25 percent precision. Both were attempts to repair an isoform dependence
that the span substrate simply does not have.

### Two things to say before they are asked

First, the substrate was never purely RNA. 403 of 540 representatives are single exon, and for those the
exon sum already is the genomic span, ratio 1.00 measured. So three quarters of comparisons were already
genomic. Going uniformly genomic removes an inherited inconsistency rather than introducing DNA.

Second, part of the gain is guaranteed rather than earned. Soto defines families by genomic sequence
identity, SD98 at fracMatch at least 0.98. Moving our substrate toward genomic sequence moves it toward
the benchmark's own definition. The honest description is RNA discovered loci, DNA compared. Detection
remains RNA native: no reads, no locus.

Third, the additive genomic span tier was already on by default and already the sole support for entire
families in the log provenance. This is a promotion of something shipped, not a new dependency.

## 2. A high precision operating point

Same substrate, identity floor raised to 0.95, measured end to end:

| setting | precision | recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| genomic span, default floors | 0.527 | 0.658 | 0.585 |
| genomic span, identity 0.95 | 0.895 | 0.446 | 0.595 |

Precision 0.895 at F1 parity. If a claim needs precision, this is the setting.

## 3. Sizes: the predictions are too small, and it is a boundary problem

Predicted copies are about half the size of the truth locus they match. Bipartite Hungarian matching on
reciprocal overlap gives median 0.54, with 104 truncated against 12 over extended. This is a paired per
locus comparison, so it is not an artifact of families being size heterogeneous.

Measured tonight, the same deficit attributed to its source:

| span source | median size / truth size | at least 0.8x | below 0.5x |
|---|---|---|---|
| representative (shipped) | 0.55 | 32% | 47% |
| union extent of reads at the locus | 11.27 | 95% | 4% |
| read extent, 3 read support | 3.79 | 87% | 10% |

The reads reach at least 0.8 of true size for 95 percent of members. The information is present. The
pipeline takes one representative out of the pile and inherits its boundaries. Single exon
representatives land at 0.29 of truth, spliced ones at 0.75, so the stub problem and the size problem are
the same problem.

The flagship cases resolve almost exactly from reads alone:

| gene | exons in rep | rep span / truth | read extent / truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| SRGAP2C | 1 | 0.03 | 1.00 |
| SRGAP2D | 1 | 0.04 | 1.02 |
| SRGAP2 | 1 | 0.05 | 1.00 |
| SRGAP2B | 1 | 0.05 | 1.25 |

The naive fix does not work: raw read extent overshoots by a median factor of 11, and 3.53 even with a
support filter, because reads spill into neighbouring transcription. The truth sits between two biased
estimators.

Bounding the locus by homology was tested and does not work either, in any of the forms tried. Bounding by
predicted family siblings is a feedback loop: it needs correct families to fix the boundaries that feed the
substrate that produces the families, and SRGAP2's four members currently sit in three predicted families,
so three of the four fell back to their representative span. Bounding by homology to any locus at all
removes that circularity and does better, but the right way to score a size ratio is the fraction of loci
at roughly correct size, and on that measure it loses:

| estimator | in 0.5 to 2x of truth | below 0.5x | above 2x |
|---|---|---|---|
| representative (shipped) | 47% | 47% | 5% |
| any locus bounded | 37% | 26% | 37% |

It converts 21 points of too small into 32 points of too big and ends up with fewer loci correct. Its
median of 1.03 against the shipped 0.55 is a distribution shift, not an accuracy gain. Varying how
alignment blocks are combined changes the result by nothing at all across a fifty fold range, so the cause
is not procedural: the read extent spills into neighbouring sequence that is itself duplicated, and asking
whether something is duplicated cannot separate this copy from the copy beside it. AMY2A is the clear case,
sitting in the amylase cluster at four to five times its true size under every variant.

Separating adjacent copies requires the exon or junction structure, which is the transcript model this was
trying to bypass. The size deficit is therefore localised but not repaired.

### The deficit is not uniform, and it is not where it matters most

Broken down per family, against RefSeq gene spans rather than duplication blocks, the aggregate 0.55 turns
out to be bimodal. Named, characterised families are close to correct:

| family | members | median size / truth | in 0.5 to 2x | stub representatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GOLGA6L | 4 | 1.00 | 4 of 4 | 0 of 4 |
| FAM72 | 4 | 0.94 | 4 of 4 | 0 of 4 |
| amylase, AMY1 and AMY2 | 4 | 0.86 | 4 of 4 | 1 of 4 |
| SEC22B | 3 | 0.77 | 2 of 3 | 1 of 3 |
| FCGR1 | 3 | 0.69 | 3 of 3 | 0 of 3 |

The families pulling the median down are almost entirely unnamed accessions, together with SRGAP2 and
NBPF: ID_212 at 0.18, ID_215 at 0.13, SRGAP2 at 0.05.

The stub column is the mechanism. Families with no single exon representatives sit near 1.0; families that
are mostly stubs sit between 0.05 and 0.21, and SRGAP2 is four stubs out of four at 0.05. Measured
separately, stub represented members are at 0.29 of true size and spliced ones at 0.75. The size problem
and the single exon representative problem are one problem, not two.

So the accurate statement is not that predicted loci are half the true size. It is that they are correctly
sized where the locus has a spliced representative, and undersized where it does not, and the second group
is the same population that limits family detection.

### Verified directly against the RefSeq annotation, using no Soto file

The table above uses a truth file we derived, in which only 222 of 362 members received a RefSeq span while
the remaining 140 kept their Soto duplication block. That is a genuine confound, so the same question was
put to the CHM13 RefSeq annotation directly. Gene spans taken from the GFF, predicted spans from the run:

| gene | RefSeq span | predicted | ratio | exons in representative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AMY1A | 9,037 | 7,781 | 0.86 | 9 |
| AMY1B | 9,039 | 7,764 | 0.86 | 9 |
| AMY1C | 9,039 | 7,779 | 0.86 | 9 |
| AMY2B | 24,891 | 25,882 | 1.04 | 12 |
| AMY2A | 8,275 | 4,329 | 0.52 | 1 |
| GOLGA6L10 | 9,483 | 9,461 | 1.00 | 9 |
| SEC22B | 25,623 | 23,325 | 0.91 | 5 |
| FCGR1A | 17,908 | 11,758 | 0.66 | 2 |
| H2BC18 | 29,671 | 15,451 | 0.52 | 1 |
| NBPF26 | 114,464 | 22,740 | 0.20 | 20 |
| SRGAP2 | 260,886 | 13,068 | 0.05 | 1 |
| SRGAP2B | 208,056 | 9,869 | 0.05 | 1 |
| SRGAP2C | 207,899 | 5,566 | 0.03 | 1 |

Every member with a spliced representative falls between 0.86 and 1.04. Every badly undersized member has a
single exon representative. The one amylase copy that is wrong, AMY2A at 0.52, is exactly the one
represented by a stub, and the three AMY1 copies agree to two decimal places at 0.86, which is a systematic
shortfall consistent with a transcript model not reaching annotated untranslated ends rather than with
noise.

Restricting the earlier per family analysis to only those members that carry a real RefSeq span, so that
every row is referenced the same way, gives the same conclusion: 77 members, spliced representatives at
median 0.77 with 69 percent in band, stub representatives at median 0.25 with 28 percent in band.

NBPF26 is the one genuine exception, at 0.20 despite a twenty exon representative, on a 114 kb gene. Not
every size failure reduces to a stub.

## 4. Answers to two specific objections

### Why several minimap2 runs

There are two categories and only one is negotiable.

The read to genome alignment must be splice aware, because IsoSeq reads cross introns and an unspliced
aligner would clip at every junction. There is one such run and it produces the BAM.

The homology runs are not a spliced and an unspliced pair. They are the same command with different seed
presets, aligning already spliced sequences. asm20 cannot seed a pair below roughly 0.82 identity, which
is its seed sensitivity envelope, so lowering its gate is a no op; a smaller seed at k equals 11 reaches
about 0.62 and recovers ancient paralogs. A third variant differs only in substrate.

On the path that defines families, dropping asm20 was verified byte identical genome wide, with asm20
contributing zero sole support edges. Adopting the genomic span as the substrate points at a single run
with one identity floor. That answers the objection by deleting the machinery rather than defending it.

### Why copy sizes are not constrained to match

Because real families are not size uniform. Measured on Soto's own annotation:

| | median smallest / largest member | below 0.5x |
|---|---|---|
| Soto families, chr1 and chr15, n=26 | 0.33 | 73% |
| Soto families, genome wide, n=76 | 0.31 | 67% |

A size uniformity constraint would reject 73 percent of real families, including SRGAP2C at 5.6 kb against
SRGAP2 at 208 kb, which is a true pair. Size is a useful diagnostic and a harmful filter.

## 5. Negative results, stated because they are load bearing

Every one of these was built, measured end to end, and rejected.

Raising the E_r identity floor from 0.70 to 0.93 was predicted offline to lift F1 to 0.553. Two pipeline
runs returned 0.433. Pooling every isoform's exons recovered about 30 true pairs and still lost to the
baseline, because new edges were only 15 to 25 percent precise. Concatenating isoforms into one sequence
cost 20 recall points. Extending the exon sum to a sharp 3 prime terminus moved 883 kb of real sequence
and changed zero family verdicts.

Relaxing the coverage floor does not help. Lowering it from 0.50 to 0.40 rescues 3 of the 180 true pairs
that have both loci detected but no edge, because 127 of those 180 have coverage exactly 0.00, meaning no
alignment record exists at all. A floor cannot admit an edge that was never computed.

Single exon representatives cannot be discarded. The input is already full length non chimeric IsoSeq, and
the unspliced reads at these loci are the same length as the spliced ones, 2329 against 2294 base pairs at
NOTCH2NLC, with under 21 bp of clipping, and they outnumber the spliced reads 4.9 to 1. An intron
retention test catches only 22 percent of them. Two of the affected truth members are histone H3 genes,
which are genuinely intronless.

### The mechanism behind most of these failures

A family must span at least two spatially distinct loci to be emitted at all. Tightening any edge rule
therefore does not merely remove edges, it removes loci, and detection falls with them. Precision rises as
intended and recall falls faster. This is why edge level improvements do not carry to pipeline output, and
why any proposal must report detection and copy count alongside precision.

## 6. One signal worth developing

Junction position co threading. Intron and exon lengths drift after duplication, but splice sites are
inherited, so each junction of one locus should land on a junction of the other when projected through the
alignment. A shared repeat or domain aligns without shared junction structure, which is exactly the false
edge the coverage floor exists to reject.

Edge level, replicated on two independent partitions. Among the pairs the coverage floor rejects, co
threading separates true from false with AUC 0.877 and 0.809, while coverage itself scores 0.093 and
0.138, meaning coverage is worse than random in that regime. A combined rule, coverage at least 0.50 or co
threading at least 0.80, adds 29 true edges for 1 false one.

The tolerance is 10 bp of alignment jitter at a splice site, not a fitted parameter.

Two limits. It is undefined for 79 percent of pairs as currently built, because it uses the
representative's exons and most representatives are single exon. Built instead on the locus junction set,
every junction at the locus carried by at least 3 reads, it becomes defined at 29 of 37 stub represented
truth loci. And the false pair counts are small, 21 overall, so the intervals are wide.

Chain shattering is why this matters. At NOTCH2NLC, 156 spliced reads produce 117 distinct junction sets,
so no chain is a usable representative, but 55 individual junctions carry at least 3 reads and 32 carry at
least 10. Shattering is combinatorial in chains and not in junctions.

## 8. Corrections made during this work

The first substrate A/B was a silent no op: a shared exon environment variable replaces the nucleotide
runs, so the genomic span sequences were built and discarded, and both arms were byte identical.

An offline replay predicted F1 0.745 for the identity 0.95 arm and the pipeline returned 0.595. The replay
consumed the complete pre gate node set, which fixed the flaw that broke the earlier identity floor
prediction, but it partitions by connected components where the pipeline runs a quasi clique
decomposition. Fixing one flaw in a proxy did not make the proxy valid.

A claim that within family size concordance detects truncation was withdrawn. Its apparent agreement with
the bipartite result was a coincidence between two quantities that do not measure the same thing, and the
control showed real families are more size heterogeneous than our predictions.

A claim that the genomic span fixes the single exon stub problem was withdrawn. Stub pairs are unchanged;
for two stubs the two substrates are identical by construction.

A claim that any locus homology bounding improves locus sizes was withdrawn. It raises the median from 0.55
to 1.03, which is what was first reported, but the median only says where the mass of the distribution
sits. Scored by the fraction of loci within 0.5 to 2 times the true size, the shipped representative span
is better, 47 percent against 37 percent. For a ratio against truth the in band fraction is the correct
statistic and the median is not.
