Defined but not fully assigned — why?

The 20 Soto families where some members are recovered and others are not. The advisor's question: you had enough to put them in one family — why not assign reads to every member?

Define (family) — uses homology

Two loci are one family iff their exon-sum sequences are homologous (asm20 identity ≥ 0.80, ≥ 2 loci). This groups similar copies. Every red member below IS in the family — homology put it there.

Assign (member) — needs separability

To recover a member you need reads that distinguish it — a base only that copy has (a PSV), or a unique mapper. Homology says "same family"; assignment needs "tell them apart." Similar ≠ separable.
The gap between the two is the whole point: a member can be homology-grouped yet have no read that separates it from its siblings. Click a family — the bar shows each missing member's reads split into unique (distinguishing) vs tied (MAPQ-0 multimappers). When the bar is all grey, homology could group it but no read can assign it — that is the K=0 identifiability floor (→ DNA). When it has green, the reads DID distinguish it — those were lost to the unspliced-seeding bug (now fixed).
