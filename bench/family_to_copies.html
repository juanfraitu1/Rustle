O1 → O2 · the hand-off

From a family to its copies

The bridge between the two graphs: a family (the nodes) becomes a set of copies, each with a reference sequence; the PSVs a read is later assigned by are the columns where they differ. Collapse those columns and you get the variation graph — copies as paths that share a backbone and bubble apart where they diverge. The reference is the assembly, not a consensus built from the reads.

reads → loci → families → copies → PSVs → variation graph → assign

Edge case · two copies that make the same isoform, far apart

If two distant paralogs produce identical transcripts, do we merge them, double-count them, or mis-assign their reads?

Definition (O1) stays correct. Family membership is transcript homology, and the grouping is chromosome- and strand-agnostic — so two distant copies making the same isoform are still one family, and they are counted as two distinct copies because a same-locus guard (prune_same_locus) only collapses transcripts that share a junction or are an unspliced span of one another. Distant loci have disjoint junctions at distinct coordinates, so one gene is never split into two, and two copies are never collapsed into one. Copy number is preserved.

Assignment (O2) runs only where copies are co-located — same chromosome, within a 5 Mb window (the hard regime where the aligner pools the reads). For copies farther apart, the aligner is the arbiter: if the transcripts are identical the reads land on both at MAPQ 0 and stay tied — never split 1/k; if the copies differ anywhere, minimap2 returns a unique best placement and the read anchors to its locus (this is exactly why separate-chromosome RABL2 needed no resolution from us).

The genuinely hard case is two copies that are both co-located and exonically identical — the K = 0 frontier: the reads span no bubble, are RNA-unresolvable and certified tied, yet χ_H still counts them as ≥ 2 copies (the chromatic number of the conflict graph). We report the copy number and abstain per-read. "Same isoform" means same junction structure — if the shared exons still carry a PSV bubble we can still assign; only identical sequence forces the tie.
