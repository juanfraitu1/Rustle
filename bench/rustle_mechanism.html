Rustle: the whole method, and every number in it

One pipeline, two mechanisms, two consequences — and a complete, source-verified list of every heuristic. Nothing withheld.

Substrate: gorilla (mGorGor1) HiFi / IsoSeq. Verification subset: the 66 Soto RNA families. Generated from real runs; regenerate with bench/mechanism/.

This page exists so that nothing about the method has to be taken on trust. It states the method in two sentences, shows the one statistical test that does the heavy lifting, names the number at every stage next to its line of source, and lists all 171 heuristics — sorted by how much each one can change a result, with the uncomfortable ones flagged, not buried.

1 · The method in two sentences

Define. Two loci are one family iff their spliced exon-sum sequences are homologous (asm20 identity ≥ 0.80, coverage-of-shorter ≥ 0.50) and there are ≥ 2 of them.

Assign. A read belongs to a copy iff it carries a base that no other copy has, beyond chance (Poisson-binomial upper tail, p < α/(n−1), α = 10⁻³). If no such base exists, the method abstains — it never guesses, and never splits a read 1/k.

That is the entire method. Everything else on this page is either (a) how those two sentences are computed, (b) two discoveries that fall out of them for free, or (c) the machinery underneath, disclosed in full. Assign is Rustle's answer to Eichler's AS>10 rule — and a stronger one: it is a calibrated, Bonferroni-corrected p-value rather than a hand-picked score, and where AS>10 is forced to call a tie wrong, the certificate abstains.

2 · The one test that does the heavy lifting

A read that spans a position where copies differ (a PSV) is evidence for one copy over the others. The certificate asks a single question: could this many matching PSVs have arisen by sequencing error alone? It is an exact Poisson-binomial upper tail (no normal approximation), compared against α/(n−1) — a Bonferroni union bound over the n−1 competitor copies.

One test, three uses — verified in source:

Stated precisely so it can’t be over-claimed: two call-sites share the literal function; the third shares the mathematical form. Not one function everywhere — one test, reused three times.

3 · Two discoveries — consequences, not new methods

Copies missing from the genome ⇐ Assign

Run the certificate in reverse: ask “do the copies I already know about explain these reads?” When a set of reads carries a co-segregating base pattern that no modeled copy explains at significance, an unmodeled copy must exist. No copy-finder was added — the same test, reading its own failures, is the detector.

Genes missing from the annotation ⇐ Define + exon-sum

A locus that (a) nobody annotated, (b) forms an exon-sum homology edge to a known family, and (c) is transcribed (reads assemble through it, ≥ GATE_MIN_READS) is a real family member — annotation or not. Similarity says it belongs; exon-sum is how similarity is measured; transcripts prove it is a real gene.

The honesty line. Detection is the consequence. Characterizing what was detected is separate machinery (e.g. hidden_copy.rs’s thresholds, the linearize permutation test) — disclosed below in the full table, never smuggled into the two-sentence story. And Define and Assign stay two ideas; they are not secretly one.

These are two mechanisms and their consequences — not four separate approaches.

4 · How the two sentences are computed — the five stages

k-mers, alignment identity, the graph, and POA are stages of one pipeline, not competing methods. Each stage below carries its one decision number and the exact line of source it lives on. (All 171 constants are in §8; these eleven are the ones that actually pick a result.)

  1. k-mer prefilter — cheap candidate generation. KMER = 18 family_detect.rs:37
  2. Exon-sum identity edge — the family edge test. id ≥ 0.80, cov ≥ 0.50 denovo_pipeline.rs:2370–71
  3. γ-quasi-clique — cluster the edges into families. γ = 0.20 family_definition.rs:173
  4. de-tie conflict graph — count copies, χ(H). de-tie δ=0.005, de_max=0.05, min_reads=3 read_conflict.rs:63
  5. Significance certificate — assign each read, or abstain. α = 10⁻³, α/(n−1) copy_assign.rs:213

Two more decision-tier numbers sit just outside the spine: the locus admission gate GATE_MIN_READS = 3 (denovo_assemble.rs:27) — the pipeline’s unit of existence — and --min-copies = 2 (copy_assign.rs:108), plus the junction bonus junction_weight = 5.0 (copy_assign.rs:210). Eleven decision numbers in total.

5 · The identifiability frontier — shown, with ground truth

The hardest question an examiner asks: “if two copies are nearly identical, does your method just merge them?” Here is the answer, run on synthetic families where the truth is known exactly. We plant a 4-copy tandem array at a ladder of divergences, and measure two things independently: does it get the copy number right, and does it assign reads or abstain?

Why this is the over-collapse rebuttal. “Over-collapse” would mean reporting fewer copies than exist — and being silently wrong. That is failing the green line. The green line never drops. What rises is the amber line: as copies approach identical, the method increasingly says “there are still N of them — I just can’t tell you which read is which.” It abstains on assignment; it never fabricates a merge. Formally, χ(H) is a lower bound on copy number (max(n_loci, χ(H)) ≤ true) — the method cannot invent a merge; the worst case is a conservative floor that it flags.

The cliff at ~97–98% is not a tuning artifact — it is the same wall that appears on real gorilla data (§7), where the remap gate sits at 98%. The synthetic frontier and the real genome agree on where identifiability ends.

6 · It genuinely recovers — not just detects

Detection could be an artifact of a permissive gate. So: delete a copy from the reference, and ask whether the certificate puts it back. (This is the inverse of the existing linearize certificate, which folds a copy in and asks if reads go unique.)

The divergent arm (V4a, ~96% identity): both deleted copies recovered at identity 1.0 across 96 PSV columns, and set_match assigned each recovered copy to its correct original (permutation-invariant matching, both pairs at 1.0) — a genuine, non-circular recovery. Recovery is at PSV-allele resolution (the copy’s distinguishing bases), which is the method’s native contract, not a full de-novo assembly.

Two disclosed design facts. (1) The experiment deletes 2 copies of a 4-copy locus, not 1 of 3 — because the absent-copy admission gate requires ≥ 3 co-varying clusters at a locus (min_clusters = 3, hardcoded in absent_copy.rs), so a single deletion from three copies can never reach it. That constraint is itself a finding, disclosed here. (2) The identical-copy arm recovers the copy number (3/3) but abstains on sequence (100% Tied, 0 PSVs) — the K=0 floor, shown rather than hidden.

7 · On real gorilla data — where the line actually falls

Two kinds of real-data evidence, and one honest boundary.

Detection works on real gorilla

The certificate already flags real reference-absent copies in gorilla IsoSeq: an honest funnel of 30 flagged → 18 surviving the editing firewall → 4 confirmed reference-absent copies, all 4 in the MHC, BLAST-corroborated. See reference_absent_copies.html (the numbers here match that page exactly). This is “copies missing from the genome ⇐ Assign,” demonstrated on real data (without an artificial deletion as ground truth).

The removal-recovery experiment is squeezed — and that is the theorem, not a defect

We scanned the gorilla catalog for a locus to run the ground-truth removal experiment on. The copy-identity distribution is bimodal: of 24 physically-tight ≥3-copy families, only 2 fall in the recoverable window [96%, 97.9%]. The rest are either <95% (separate loci, not collapsed) or ≥98% (too similar to clear the remap gate). Recovery needs copies divergent enough to clear the 98% full-span remap_max_identity gate and similar enough to collapse into ≥3 clusters — a narrow band that recent gorilla segdups mostly sit above. And the two families that did land in-window failed for reasons unrelated to divergence: one had only 3 supported copies (the same hardcoded min_clusters=3 floor as §6), the other carried a mis-chained 492 kb annotation artifact — so the empty result is part information-theoretic (the bimodal squeeze) and part engineering (two hardcoded gates), and both are disclosed rather than folded into “the theorem.”

Could a better method recover the ≥98% cases? The honest answer splits by regime:

RegimeRecoverable from RNA reads?
Identical over covered basesNo method can — it is a theorem. Reads from the two copies are draws from the same distribution; MCC = χ(H) gives them the same color. We are provably as good as anyone: everyone gets copy-number-from-depth, no separation.
Near-identical, sparse PSVs (~98–99.9%)Possible in principle, but the recoverable count is fixed by the PSVs in the reads (χ(H)), not the algorithm. Our certificate is a near-optimal (likelihood-ratio) use of those PSVs. Recovering more needs orthogonal data — DNA read-depth (SDA / Vollger / Bailey) — which the framework routes to (K=0 → DNA).
Divergent (<98% full-span)We recover it — §5 and §6, on ground truth.

Against the RNA competitors: Eichler’s AS>10 would force a wrong call in the near-identical band; longcallR is unique-mapper-only and discards exactly the multimappers we use. Where they false-positive, we abstain. The one caveat we hold to honestly: “near-optimal use of the RNA PSVs” — a joint haplotype-assembly method might squeeze marginally more from the same reads; the frontier (χ(H)) is the theorem, and the gap to identical-copy recovery is information-theoretic, requiring DNA.

So the real-gorilla removal-recovery returns no positive — and that is the expected result, corroborating the existing figure that ~15–17% of copies fall to K=0 and are routed to DNA. Sim (§5–6) proves the mechanism with ground truth; the O4 catalog proves it fires on real data; the theorem explains the boundary between them.
