What is a γ-quasi-clique?

A family is a dense cluster in the homology graph (nodes = gene loci, an edge = "these two are homologous"). "Dense" is tuned by one number, γ — and clique, quasi-clique, and connected component are the same rule at three settings of γ. Drag it and watch the same graph regroup.

γ = 0.40 a group is a family iff its edge density ≥ γ

Why the middle

clique (γ=1) is too strict: one missing homology edge (A1–A5 here) shatters a real family.

connected component (γ→0) is too loose: a single shared repeat drags 5 unrelated genes into one false family — this is the real over-merge (a 728-member blob spanning chr1→chrY).

γ-quasi-clique (γ≈0.4) keeps a cohesive family whole even with a few missing edges, yet a sparse repeat-bridge fails the density gate and drops out. Cohesive, not brittle; no arbitrary "≥ X% identical" cutoff.
